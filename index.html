How a turn works

A turn draws the edges allowed for it (default two; your very first turn of the game draws one). Each edge starts at one of your own posts and reaches any lattice node within your reach box — diagonals included. You may cut at most one rival edge per stroke, never one that is still glowing (drawn last turn, or an opening stick during the first round). Every time a player runs out of actions, everyone banks their whole live area into their score. After the edge cap, the highest banked score wins.

Enclose

A territory game on a lattice. Draw fences from your own posts, close loops to enclose land, and cut rival fences to steal it. Land you hold is banked into your score every time any player finishes a turn — so holding ground early pays off again and again.

Making a move. Click one of your posts (the big dots of your colour), then click a glowing target — or drag from post to target. An edge may reach any node within your reach box around the start post. You cannot overlap your own fence or hop over your own posts, and each stroke may cut at most one rival fence.

Fresh fences glow. A fence drawn this turn (and through every rival's next turn) cannot be cut. Opening sticks glow through the first round too — nobody can kill a starting stick before its owner's first turn. Plan around it: close a pen, and rivals must wait before snipping it open.

Scoring. When a player runs out of actions for their turn, every player banks their current enclosed area. After everyone's edge allowance is spent the highest banked total wins; equal tops draw. A seat with no legal edge passes automatically.

Enclaves (the confusing bit, clarified). A closed ring that sits wholly inside a rival's bigger ring is an enclave. Your own nested rings never double-count — only your outer ring scores, and its area already includes the inner ground. Cross-player pockets work like this: Enclaves box ticked (default): the pocket keeps scoring for its builder and the outer ring's area still covers that same ground, so one pocket can score twice for two players. Enclaves box cleared: the outermost ring claims everything it surrounds — any rival pocket inside your ring scores zero for its builder, while your own total is unchanged (it already covered that ground). Nothing about your fences changes either way, only the numbers each pocket is worth.

Poison spots. Tick Poison spots and 3–6 purple spots appear each new match. Close a ring around one and that whole pen turns against you: it scores negative from then on — a clean +64 pen around a spot becomes −64, which can eat back into what you've already banked, but your total is never allowed below 0 — and it is contagious: a rival whose bigger ring swallows your infected pocket gets infected too. Two spots in one ring only flip it once — a spot your fence merely grazes still counts (touching is enough) — and spots never block fences. The AI avoids them as much as you should.

Boost spots. Tick Boost spots and 2–6 green spots appear each new match. Close a ring around one and that pen scores 50% more — +64 becomes +96 — stacking multiplicatively per spot (two spots ×2.25, rounded). Poison still trumps: a pen that contains a purple spot stays negative and ignores the green ones. Like poison, spots never block fences; the AI plays for the boost. On the board a boosted pen's shading tints green and an infected pen's purple — on top of its owner's colour.

Variants. 2 or 4 players, board size, reach, board shape (square / octagon — the grid stays the same, off-shape nodes simply sit out), edges per turn and the per-player edge allowance (40–100 each, defaulting to 60 / 80 / 90 for 2 / 3 / 4 players) all live in the Game Controls card — hover it or press C to open; changing any setting starts a new game. Three scoring/board toggles live there too: Barriers scatters a couple of compact blocked “rocks” over the lattice every new match (no fence may end on, pass over, or touch them — but land you enclose around them still counts normally), Enclaves switches between the two pocket rules explained under Scoring above, and Poison spots / Boost spots scatter the purple hazards and green bonuses explained just above. The Choose players panel (hover it or press A) sits right under Game Controls and assigns Human or AI (Easy/Normal/Hard) to every seat — mix freely (any Human seat pauses the game on it), set every seat to AI and watch them play each other; pressing A on a fully-AI table grabs seat 1 for you. The eval bar splits one bar into every seat's share of the board, in any mode.
